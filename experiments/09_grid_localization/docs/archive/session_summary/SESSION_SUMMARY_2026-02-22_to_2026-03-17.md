## Phase 1: Performance Optimization & Scalability (Feb 22–25)

### Batch Prediction Speedups

All models were rewritten to predict in a single vectorized call instead of per-sample loops:

| Model | Before | After | Speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random Forest eval | 175.6 s | 0.107 s | **1,647×** |
| XGBoost eval | 7.8 s | 0.110 s | **71×** |
| Gaussian h=3 eval | ~600 s | 1.75 s | **~340×** |
| MLP eval | ~300 s | ~0.5 s | **~600×** |

Key technique for Gaussian: pre-enumerate all valid h-step paths as numpy arrays, then broadcast `norm.logpdf` across `(N_samples, N_paths, H)` tensor in a single call.
Key technique for sklearn models: batch all feature vectors, call `classifier.predict(X)` once.

Net result: full 7×7 experiment matrix (71 configs) dropped from 8+ hours to ~10 minutes.

## Phase 2: Voronoi Feature Experiments & Regression (Feb 26 – Mar 1)

### AoA Dominance — 10×10 Voronoi Classification

Running XGBoost on `sim_data_voronoi_2026-02-25_23-17-56`:

| Features | h=0 acc | h=1 acc | MAE |
|---|---|---|---|
| RSS | 18.8% | 23.2% | 5.70 m |
| RSS + SINR | 18.8% | 23.0% | 5.72 m |
| + AoA azimuth | 69.6% | 77.9% | 0.58 m |
| **+ AoA elevation** | **84.6%** | **88.5%** | **0.25 m** |
| CQI only | 1.3% | 1.3% | 9.11 m |

**Key insight:** RSS ≈ SINR with no interferers (single BS, distance-ring ambiguity). AoA breaks the 360° symmetry with a +52 pp jump. Elevation adds 9 pp more (encodes distance to BS). CQI is useless at close BS range (all UEs saturate top quantization level).

**Best result:** XGBoost, RSS+SINR+AoA_az+AoA_el, h=1 → **88.5% accuracy, 0.25m MAE**

### Per-Cell Analysis (10×10 single-user, XGBoost h=1, RSS+SINR+AoA_az+AoA_el)

| Voronoi Cell | Channel | Grid pts | Accuracy | Cross-cell confusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Park | UMi_LOS | 101 | 95.0% | 3.4% |
| Residential | Mixed | 35 | 92.2% | 5.1% |
| Highway | RMa_LOS | 26 | 73.4% | 27% |
| Shopping center | UMi_NLOS | 8 | 65.8% | 36% |

Highway and shopping center are worst — both have small cell sizes (26 and 8 grid points) leading to high cross-cell confusion, and RMa_LOS produces a featureless flat fingerprint with no rich multipath. Large cells (park, residential) are well-classified even at cell boundaries. Note: this 10×10 setup has **no interferers** — single serving BS only.

### OOM Fix — Gaussian Model on Large Grids

**Error:** `numpy._core._exceptions._ArrayMemoryError: Unable to allocate 6.43 GiB` for h=3 on 10×10.
**Root cause:** `norm.logpdf` broadcast `(N=8000, P=35952, H=3)` = 862M float64 values in one shot.
**Fix:** Chunked batch processing in `GaussianTransitionModel.predict_batch()`:
```python
_MEM_BUDGET = 256 * 1024 * 1024  # 256 MB per chunk
chunk_size = max(1, int(_MEM_BUDGET / (P * history_len * 8)))
```
Verified working on 10×10 h=3 and 15×15 h=1 with no memory errors.

## Phase 3: Multi-User Heterogeneous Experiment (Mar 1–10)

### Setup

5 users simulated simultaneously on a 15×15 Voronoi grid, each with a different device profile:

| User | Device | Rx antennas | Antenna gain | Height |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | Flagship A | 4 | 0 dB | 1.5 m |
| U2 | Mid-range | 2 | -2 dB | 1.5 m |
| U3 | Budget/Old | 1 | -4 dB | 1.5 m |
| U4 | Flagship B | 4 | 0 dB | 1.5 m |
| U5 | Tablet/IoT | 2 | -1 dB | 0.9 m |

U1 and U4 share hardware parameters but different walk seeds — isolates device effect from trajectory.

- **Total samples:** 450,005 (5 users × ~90k each)
- **Classes:** 225 grid points
- **Split:** 80/20 chronological per user (no leakage)
- **Models:** XGBoost and Random Forest

### Experiment Design (E1–E7)

| Exp | Features | h | AoA | Device params |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | RSS + SINR | 0 | No | No |
| E2 | RSS + SINR stacked | 3 | No | No |
| E3 | E2 + device params | 3 | No | Yes (n_ant, gain, height) |
| E4 | E2 + user_id | 3 | No | Oracle ID |
| E5 | RSS + SINR + AoA | 0 | Yes | No |
| E6 | RSS + SINR + AoA stacked | 3 | Yes | No |
| E7 | E6 + device params | 3 | Yes | Yes |

E1/E2 test core transition hypothesis; E3/E4 probe heterogeneity correction; E5–E7 add AoA; E7 is the full-featured upper bound.

### Overall Results

| Experiment | XGBoost acc | XGBoost MAE | RF acc | RF MAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 (static, no AoA) | 27.3% | 8.39 m | 43.5% | 5.81 m |
| E2 (transitions h=3) | 50.4% | 4.53 m | 52.5% | 4.01 m |
| E3 (+ device params) | 58.3% | 3.20 m | 61.2% | 2.69 m |

## Key Findings Summary

1. **Transition history is universal** — improves every algorithm at every grid size, +10 to +29 pp accuracy, +24–55% MAE reduction. Holds even for the simple Gaussian model.

2. **AoA is the dominant feature** — alone adds ~52 pp over RSS+SINR on BS-inside-grid scenarios. Elevation adds ~9 pp more (encodes distance). With a BS at grid center, angular discrimination makes localization highly accurate.

3. **RSS ≈ SINR when there is a single BS** — combining them adds nothing. Both encode distance-ring information without interferers. AoA breaks the rotational symmetry.

4. **Device heterogeneity is partially corrected by device params** — adding n_antennas, antenna_gain_db, ue_height as features (E3) gives +7–8 pp over transitions alone (E2). User_id oracle (E4) gives essentially the same gain — the continuous device params carry most of the information.

5. **AoA features generalize across devices** — E6 cross-user accuracy is 76.7% (XGBoost), dropping only ~7 pp from pooled. E2 (RSS/SINR only) drops ~4 pp but from a much lower baseline.

6. **Weaker devices benefit more from AoA** — U2 (2-ant, -2 dB) and U5 (tablet, low height) show the largest accuracy gains when AoA is added (+41–42 pp in E6 vs E2), since angle information compensates for degraded received-power measurements.

7. **Cell environment matters** — Park (UMi_LOS) consistently achieves highest RSS/SINR accuracy; highway (RMa_LOS) is worst but gets the biggest AoA boost (+38.6 pp) since its flat LOS fingerprint provides no spatial discrimination without angle information.

8. **Classification beats regression** — at every tested grid size and feature set, discrete classification outperforms continuous regression by 1.3–1.6× in MAE terms.
