The controlled grid was simulated under three propagation settings, each with the BS at the grid center and beyond its corner: RMa line of sight for every walk, a mixed setting in which each Voronoi cell has its own UMi line-of-sight or non-line-of-sight scenario, and uniform UMi non-line-of-sight. Without AoA, adding three steps of history to RSS and SINR raised exact-cell accuracy in all twelve configurations (three propagation settings, two placements, two classifiers; Figure~\ref{fig:cg-sets}). XGBoost gained between $13.2$ and $27.4$ percentage points, which is $2.6$ to $4.3\,\text{m}$ less mean error, and Random Forest between $4.9$ and $13.0$ points, or $1.0$ to $3.1\,\text{m}$. A fourth set, uniform NLOS at $3.5\,\text{GHz}$, agrees with the $3.0\,\text{GHz}$ one within one to two points, so the carrier does not matter here. The absolute accuracy, in contrast, moves by tens of points with the propagation: with the BS at the center, XGBoost with history reaches $29.6\%$ under uniform NLOS, $50.6\%$ under RMa line of sight and $59.1\%$ under the mixed setting. The snapshot models are weak and differ between classifiers (mixed setting, center BS: $31.7\%$ for XGBoost and $53.5\%$ for Random Forest), so part of XGBoost's larger gain is recovery from a poor snapshot model; the gains of both classifiers are positive in every set. Always guessing the center of the grid gives a mean error of $10.9\,\text{m}$ on the test samples.

The placement change that leaves history's contribution positive removes most of AoA's. With history already in the input, adding AoA raised XGBoost's accuracy by $20.9$, $19.5$ and $38.5$ points at the center BS in the RMa, mixed and NLOS settings, but by only $4.0$, $1.0$ and $6.2$ points at the corner, and Random Forest's by $13.7$, $9.9$ and $31.9$ against $2.8$, $-0.4$ and $5.8$ points. From the corner the whole grid falls within a wedge of about $52^\circ$, and once a $5^\circ$ codebook and a few degrees of estimation noise are applied, azimuth no longer separates neighboring cells. The remaining gain at the corner is consistent with elevation, which encodes distance, but this grid study does not isolate it. With AoA available, history adds less at the center BS ($0.2$ to $0.6\,\text{m}$) than at the corner ($0.7$ to $2.0\,\text{m}$), where the angle is weaker and leaves more error to average away. This loss of AoA's value is specific to a short-range grid with $2\,\text{m}$ cells; it is not a general property of corner placements.

Where history was swept one step at a time, most of the gain arrived with the first step~(Figure~\ref{fig:cg-depth}). On a single-user $7\times7$ grid, one step of history raised accuracy by $10$ to $12$ points for each of XGBoost, Random Forest and a multilayer perceptron, and the next two steps added about $5$ to $7$ points between them. With AoA available, the same profile appears in a uniform urban-macro NLOS scenario, where accuracy rose from $29\%$ to $46\%$ at the first step and to $51\%$ by $h=3$. In the corresponding LOS scenario, where AoA alone already placed $93\%$ of samples in the right cell, history added $3$ points at the first step and nothing after it.

The direction of the history effect is also stable across scale and estimator~(Figure~\ref{fig:cg-scale}). In the single-user version of the study, history raised accuracy at every grid size from $3\times3$ to $20\times20$ cells, by between $9.2$ and $21.8$ points, and on the $7\times7$ grid it raised the accuracy of all four estimators tried, from $4.7$ points for a Gaussian likelihood model to $18.2$ points for XGBoost.

Longer windows separate the model families (Figure~\ref{fig:history-extended}). The tree ensembles take their gain at one to three steps and then stop: XGBoost is flat from $h=3$ to $h=15$ (mixed setting, corner BS, RSS and SINR: $3.58\,\text{m}$ to $3.46\,\text{m}$), and Random Forest is best at three to five steps and worse at fifteen ($2.78\,\text{m}$ to $3.35\,\text{m}$). A one-dimensional CNN and a GRU keep improving with every added depth: the CNN goes from $8.34\,\text{m}$ at $h=0$ to $2.49\,\text{m}$ at $h=15$ and the GRU to $1.42\,\text{m}$, against $3.35$ and $3.46\,\text{m}$ for the trees. With AoA the ordering is the same, with the GRU at $1.30\,\text{m}$ against $2.82$ to $3.15\,\text{m}$ for the trees; with the BS at the center the GRU reaches $0.60\,\text{m}$ against $0.99\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost. The same ordering holds in all four sets tested. History therefore helps every family, but the neural models use a longer window than the trees can, and the final ranking of the families at long history is the reverse of the ranking at no history.

Two controls point to what the neural models use. Giving the trees only the current report and the mean of the window, which is what an averaging account of history would predict to be enough, does not make them keep improving: without AoA they are worse than the trees on raw lags from $h=3$ on (mixed setting, corner BS, $h=5$: XGBoost $4.52\,\text{m}$ against $3.51\,\text{m}$), and with AoA they only equal them. And permuting the reports inside each window, in training and in test, removes the advantage of the networks in every set: without AoA the mixed-setting GRU at $h=15$ goes from $1.42\,\text{m}$ to $5.53\,\text{m}$, and it gets worse, not better, as the window grows. The most economical reading is that, on this grid, a single RSS and SINR report is ambiguous between cells that lie on the same signal contour around the BS, that the mean of a window does not remove that ambiguity, and that the ordered sequence of reports, which traces the movement across the contours, does. The history gain on the grid would then have two parts: an averaging part, shared by all models and the only part isolated in the macro-cell study, and a sequence part that only recurrent and convolutional models exploit. This is an interpretation supported by four sets and two seeds in the shuffled test, not a direct proof, and the shuffled networks are worse than a hand-made mean feature, so the shuffled result is not a clean mean-only bound.

Because RSS and SINR are stacked in absolute form, part of what a history window carries is a stable, device-specific offset, and history could in principle be helping only by identifying the device. Supplying the device parameters directly rules this out as the whole story. In the mixed setting with the BS at the center, the parameters alone raise XGBoost from $31.7\%$ to $59.2\%$, as much as history does ($59.1\%$), and the user identifier alone is as informative ($61.8\%$); but history still adds on top of them, $7.5$ points for XGBoost and $6.1$ for Random Forest ($59.2\%$ to $66.7\%$ and $65.7\%$ to $71.8\%$). With AoA available, history adds less ($2.2$ points for XGBoost on top of the device parameters).

Every number in this section comes from a single run unless stated, and the same configuration evaluated in two separate runs differed by $2$ to $3$ points in an earlier version of this study, so only differences well above that are read as effects. The depth and scale sweeps above come from earlier single-user simulations that were not regenerated. The controlled grid also tests later time steps of users seen in training, at no more than $61\,\text{m}$ from the BS. Whether history keeps its value at macro-cell range, for users the model has never seen and a population with mixed hardware, is the question the macro-cell study answers.
