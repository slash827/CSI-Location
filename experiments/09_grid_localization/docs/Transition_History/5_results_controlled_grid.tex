\section{Results: The Controlled Grid}
\label{sec:results-controlled-grid}

% src: technical_documentation.md §7.2.1 (center BS, XGB/RF), §7.3.1-7.3.3 (corner BS); RF corner snapshot = 88.1 - 5.9 pp (documented gain, §7.3.2)
Without AoA, adding three steps of history to RSS and SINR raised exact-cell accuracy in every configuration of the controlled-grid study, for both classifiers and both BS placements~(Figure~\ref{fig:cg-placement}). The size of the gain depends strongly on the classifier. Random Forest gained $8.5$ percentage points with the BS at the center~($46.0\%$ to $54.5\%$) and $5.9$ points with the BS at the corner. XGBoost gained $22.4$ and $28.8$ points, but much of that is recovery from an unusually weak snapshot model: without history XGBoost reached only $28.6\%$ against Random Forest's $46.0\%$ at the center, and $57.2\%$ against about $82\%$ at the corner. Random Forest is therefore the better guide to how much history adds in this setting, and the direction of the effect, rather than its size, is the result.

The same placement change that leaves history's contribution positive removes almost all of AoA's. With history already in the input, adding AoA raised XGBoost's accuracy by $31.4$ points at the center BS but by only $2.3$ points at the corner, and Random Forest's by $25.7$ and $1.7$ points. From the corner the whole grid falls within a wedge of about $52^\circ$, and once a $5^\circ$ codebook and a few degrees of estimation noise are applied, azimuth no longer separates neighboring cells. The corner placement also widens the span of distances across the grid to $21$--$61\,\text{m}$, and with it the span of RSS values, so that both classifiers reach over $85\%$ accuracy there from RSS, SINR and history alone. This loss of AoA's value is specific to a short-range grid with $2\,\text{m}$ cells; it is not a general property of corner placements.

Where history was swept one step at a time, most of the gain arrived with the first step~(Figure~\ref{fig:cg-depth}). On a single-user $7\times7$ grid, one step of history raised accuracy by $10$ to $12$ points for each of XGBoost, Random Forest and a multilayer perceptron, and the next two steps added about $5$ to $7$ points between them. With AoA available, the same profile appears in a uniform urban-macro NLOS scenario, where accuracy rose from $29\%$ to $46\%$ at the first step and to $51\%$ by $h=3$. In the corresponding LOS scenario, where AoA alone already placed $93\%$ of samples in the right cell, history added $3$ points at the first step and nothing after it.

The direction of the history effect is also stable across scale and estimator~(Figure~\ref{fig:cg-scale}). In the single-user version of the study, history raised accuracy at every grid size from $3\times3$ to $20\times20$ cells, by between $9.2$ and $21.8$ points, and on the $7\times7$ grid it raised the accuracy of all four estimators tried, from $4.7$ points for a Gaussian likelihood model to $18.2$ points for XGBoost.

Because RSS and SINR are stacked in absolute form, part of what a history window carries is a stable, device-specific offset, and history could in principle be helping only by identifying the device. Supplying the device parameters directly rules this out as the whole story: with antenna count, gain and height already in the input, history still added $8.4$ points for XGBoost and $6.0$ for Random Forest. With AoA available at the center BS, by contrast, history added almost nothing, $1.5$ points for XGBoost and $-0.7$ for Random Forest, since a classifier that already reaches above $80\%$ from AoA has little ambiguity left for history to resolve.

% src: technical_documentation.md §7.2.1 vs §7.2.6 (BASE 28.6 vs 25.7; BASE_H3 51.0 vs 48.9)
Every number in this section comes from a single run, and the same configuration evaluated in two separate runs differs by $2$ to $3$ points, so only differences well above that are read as effects. The controlled grid also tests later time steps of users seen in training, at no more than $61\,\text{m}$ from the BS. Whether history keeps its value at macro-cell range, for users the model has never seen and a population with mixed hardware, is the question the macro-cell study answers.
