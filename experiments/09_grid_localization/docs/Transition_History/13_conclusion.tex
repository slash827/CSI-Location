\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% DRAFT for author review.
In a simulated single-BS macro cell, five steps of transition history lower the position error of devices that report an angle of arrival by $15$ to $21\%$ in all seven model families tested, and the choice of model matters far less than the input. The gain comes from the angle channels and is the averaging of noisy angle reports, chiefly elevation, which shows up as lower range error; it vanishes with exact angles and shrinks to $2$ to $6\%$ when the angle error persists across reports. Devices without an angle gain no more than about $1\%$ on uniform NLOS, where they are barely better than a constant guess, and about $1.2\,\text{m}$ under mixed propagation for tree ensembles. Line-of-sight propagation, interfering base stations and a blocked line of sight do not change the multi-antenna result.

Three follow-ups would settle what remains. Measured angle-of-arrival traces would show how correlated real angle errors are, which sets where the gain lies between the independent and persistent cases. Simulations with variable step length or sampling rate would separate the number of reports from the distance they cover. A direct examination of what the tree ensembles infer from RSS history under mixed propagation would test the account offered for the single-antenna gain.
