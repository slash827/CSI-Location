\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

% src: FINAL_REPORT.md §9 items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; §10 item 6 (device groups imposed at feature level); model_zoo.py train_deep (test-based stopping); technical_documentation.md §7.2.1 vs §7.2.6 (single-run variability)
All results come from simulation. Spatial consistency, the property that gives a measurement sequence its information, is a feature of the channel model here; real channels exhibit it, but also add non-stationarity, hardware impairments and calibration drift that the simulations omit. The trajectories are likewise synthetic: they are generated on a grid graph rather than drawn from measured traces, and lack the road geometry and stop-and-go behavior of real movement.

The two device groups of the macro-cell study share one simulated channel, since every UE is simulated with the same omnidirectional array and the absence of an angle is imposed when features are built. The contrast between the groups is therefore a contrast in available measurements rather than in physical hardware. The $85/15$ population mix is an assumption, which is why every result is reported per group rather than for the blend. Each test split of $47$ users contains only $3$ to $11$ single-antenna devices, so their errors carry much wider seed-to-seed uncertainty than those of multi-antenna devices, and any result resting on the nearest range band rests on a few hundred single-antenna samples at most.

The angle-channel result rests on $20$ user splits for XGBoost and Random Forest and on five for $k$-NN and the four neural models. The central result also depends on the simulated angle error: every report carries an independent $4^\circ$ Gaussian error, quantized to $5^\circ$ steps, and Section~\ref{subsec:averaging} shows that the gain falls from about $17\%$ to $2$ to $6\%$ when that error persists across reports. How correlated the errors of a real angle estimator are was not measured, and the correlated case was run for the trees at four correlations and for the neural models at one. The single-antenna gain that the tree ensembles obtain from RSS history under mixed propagation is linked to the propagation state by an oracle experiment, not by an examination of what the models infer, and five splits cannot settle whether the neural models share it. The interfering base stations were switched off but not moved.

Because every step of a walk advances exactly one grid cell, history depth measures both the number of samples and the distance travelled, and the two cannot be separated in these data; doing so requires simulations with variable step length or variable sampling rate. The neural models chose their stopping epoch on the test users, which flatters their absolute errors and any comparison with the tree and instance-based models, though not the comparisons between depths of one model. Finally, the controlled-grid results are single runs, and repeated runs of the same configuration differ by two to three percentage points, so only their larger effects are interpreted.
