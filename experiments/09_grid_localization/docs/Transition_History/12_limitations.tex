\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

% src: FINAL_REPORT.md §9 items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; §10 item 6 (device groups imposed at feature level); model_zoo.py train_deep (test-based stopping); technical_documentation.md §7.2.1 vs §7.2.6 (single-run variability)
All results come from simulation. Spatial consistency, the property that gives a measurement sequence its information, is a feature of the channel model here; real channels exhibit it, but also add non-stationarity, hardware impairments and calibration drift that the simulations omit. The trajectories are likewise synthetic: they are generated on a grid graph rather than drawn from measured traces, and lack the road geometry and stop-and-go behavior of real movement.

The two device groups of the macro-cell study share one simulated channel, since every UE is simulated with the same omnidirectional array and the absence of an angle is imposed when features are built. The contrast between the groups is therefore a contrast in available measurements rather than in physical hardware. The $85/15$ population mix is an assumption, which is why every result is reported per group rather than for the blend. Each test split of $47$ users contains only $3$ to $11$ single-antenna devices, so their errors carry much wider seed-to-seed uncertainty than those of multi-antenna devices, and any result resting on the nearest range band rests on a few hundred single-antenna samples at most.

The angle-channel result rests on $20$ user splits for XGBoost and Random Forest and on five for $k$-NN and the four neural models. The central result also depends on the simulated angle error: every report carries an independent $4^\circ$ Gaussian error, quantized to $5^\circ$ steps, and Section~\ref{subsec:averaging} shows that the gain falls from about $17\%$ to $2$ to $6\%$ when that error persists across reports. How correlated the errors of a real angle estimator are was not measured, and the correlated case was run for the trees at four correlations and for the neural models at one. The single-antenna gain that the tree ensembles obtain from RSS history under mixed propagation is linked to the propagation state by an oracle experiment, not by an examination of what the models infer, and five splits cannot settle whether the neural models share it. The interfering base stations were switched off but not moved.

Because every step of a walk advances exactly one grid cell, history depth measures both the number of samples and the distance travelled, and the two cannot be separated in these data; doing so requires simulations with variable step length or variable sampling rate. The neural models chose their stopping epoch on the test users, which flatters their absolute errors and any comparison with the tree and instance-based models, though not the comparisons between depths of one model. Finally, the controlled-grid tree results are single runs, and repeated runs of the same configuration differ by two to three percentage points, so only their larger effects are interpreted; the CNN and GRU curves rest on three seeds and the shuffled-window GRU on two. The neural stopping remark above concerns the macro-cell study; on the controlled grid and in the $25\times25$ runs the epoch is chosen on a validation block taken from the training data.

The absolute controlled-grid numbers depend strongly on the propagation setting. The grid was regenerated after two defects of the generator were found, a carrier frequency that was never applied and Voronoi cells that never changed the propagation scenario, and it was simulated under three settings: rural-macro line of sight, a per-cell mix of urban-micro line of sight and NLOS, and uniform NLOS. With the BS beyond the grid corner, XGBoost with three steps of history and no angle reaches $48.6$, $57.3$ and $25.8\%$ exact-cell accuracy under the three settings. The direction of every history effect is the same in all three, but no single absolute level should be read as the accuracy of the method. The earlier controlled-grid simulations, which gave higher accuracies, cannot be regenerated from the repository and are not used.

The sequence finding of the controlled grid is limited in three ways. First, it was tested on the grid and in the $25\times25$ regression runs but not in the macro-cell study; at macro range the gain is still explained by the averaging of angle reports, and whether order carries information there is open. Second, shuffling the reports inside the window removes both their temporal pattern and the knowledge of which report is the newest, so the test cannot separate a trend from recency. Third, the window-mean control rules out one explanation for the plateau of the tree ensembles, a missing average, but does not identify the cause; overfitting to many correlated lag columns remains possible. Finally, the studies do not share one angle-error model: the controlled grid uses an error that grows as the SINR falls, while the macro-cell study and the $25\times25$ runs cited here use a fixed $4^\circ$ error, and the $25\times25$ results with an angle change markedly between the two models.

The $25\times25$ regression runs pool five device profiles in one model and are single runs per propagation setting. The profiles are not equally easy to locate: in the line-of-sight set one profile has a mean error of $13.7\,\text{m}$ with history and an angle, about twice the $6.4$ to $7.4\,\text{m}$ of the others, so the pooled error hides differences between devices.
