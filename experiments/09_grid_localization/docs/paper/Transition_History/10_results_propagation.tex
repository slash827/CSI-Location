\section{Results: Propagation, Blockage and Interference}
\label{sec:results-propagation}

\subsection{Mixed LOS and NLOS Propagation}
\label{subsec:mixed}

% src: mixed_propagation_2026_09/seed_repeats_all_models_summary.json (verdict: overall paired gains 11.4-15.4%, all significant; per-run multi/single maes for paired per-group gains; ranking h=5 16.98-17.34 for ranks 1-6); history_gain_by_zone_summary.json (h=0 test samples LOS 21,866 / NLOS 7,938); followup_2026_10/mixed_propagation (20 seeds: single full XGB +1.14+-0.29, RF +1.28+-0.37)
Giving the four regions of the macro cell their own LOS or NLOS propagation leaves the main result intact~(Figure~\ref{fig:mixed-gain}). About three quarters of test samples now fall in LOS regions. History lowered the overall error of all seven models, by $11$ to $15\%$, with every seed-paired confidence interval clear of zero. For multi-antenna devices the gains are $2.3$ to $3.9\,\text{m}$, as on the uniform-NLOS data, and the six trained models other than $k$-NN again lie within $0.4\,\text{m}$ of each other. The single-antenna result differs: both tree ensembles now gain about $1.6\,\text{m}$ for single-antenna devices over five splits and $1.1$ to $1.3\,\text{m}$ over $20$, while the neural models and $k$-NN do not gain beyond their intervals.

Signal strength is more informative about range in the LOS regions. A path-loss fit to the LOS samples gives $\gamma = 2.58$ with a shadowing spread of $7.7\,\text{dB}$, and range explains $26\%$ of the variance in RSS. In the NLOS regions the fit gives $\gamma = 3.60$, a spread of $11.9\,\text{dB}$, and only $7\%$ of the variance explained.

% src: mixed_propagation_2026_09/history_gain_by_zone_summary.json verdict (LOS 15.94%, NLOS 8.65%; XGBoost, seed 42) and ablations_2026_09/history_gain_by_zone_summary.json (uniform NLOS, same regions: 15.98%, 4.84%); followup_2026_10/mixed_propagation/region_gain_did_vs_uniform.json and summary.<model>.full.multi.gain_vs_snap.geo_LOS/geo_NLOS (20 seeds, Student-t: XGB uniform 3.56+-0.17 / 1.75+-0.16, mixed 3.10+-0.19 / 1.55+-0.13; gap 1.81+-0.27 vs 1.54+-0.24, mixed minus uniform -0.27+-0.25; RF full gap difference -0.01+-0.18; angle_hist XGB -0.16+-0.28, RF -0.08+-0.17)
History helps more in the regions that carry LOS propagation, but this is a property of the regions and not of line-of-sight propagation~(Figure~\ref{fig:los-nlos}). In a single XGBoost run, history lowered error by $15.9\%$ in the two LOS regions and by $8.7\%$ in the two NLOS regions. The uniform-NLOS realization of the same walks contains no LOS at all, and the same regions give $16.0\%$ and $4.8\%$. Over $20$ splits, for multi-antenna devices, XGBoost's gain in the LOS regions is $3.56 \pm 0.17\,\text{m}$ on uniform NLOS and $3.10 \pm 0.19\,\text{m}$ with real LOS, and in the NLOS regions $1.75 \pm 0.16$ and $1.55 \pm 0.13\,\text{m}$. The gap between regions is $1.81 \pm 0.27\,\text{m}$ without any LOS and $1.54 \pm 0.24\,\text{m}$ with it, a difference of $-0.27 \pm 0.25\,\text{m}$, and Random Forest gives $-0.01 \pm 0.18\,\text{m}$. Making the propagation real did not widen the gap. The regions differ in range and in the error left to remove, since the gain is largest in the nearest and farthest range bands~(Section~\ref{sec:results-mechanism}), and these are where the LOS regions lie. Earlier readings of the zone comparison as support for a LOS effect should therefore be discarded.

The single-antenna gain from RSS history on these data is not explained by the cleaner LOS path-loss relation: over five splits XGBoost's RSS-only gain is $1.28 \pm 0.42\,\text{m}$ for samples in LOS regions and $1.53 \pm 0.87\,\text{m}$ for samples in NLOS regions. What it does depend on is the propagation state itself. A single-antenna snapshot cannot tell LOS from NLOS, which differ by about $17\,\text{dB}$ in RSS, and adding the true LOS flag to the snapshot as an oracle lowers error by $3.27 \pm 0.49\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost and $3.04 \pm 0.44\,\text{m}$ for Random Forest over $20$ splits. RSS history recovers part of this, $1.28 \pm 0.29$ and $1.41 \pm 0.36\,\text{m}$, and given on top of the oracle flag it still gains $1.01 \pm 0.27$ and $0.77 \pm 0.25\,\text{m}$. That remaining gain is concentrated in the $14\%$ of windows that span a region crossing, where it is $2.6$ and $3.9\,\text{m}$, against $0.8$ and $0.3\,\text{m}$ for windows entirely in LOS regions and $0.6$ and $0.0\,\text{m}$ for windows entirely in NLOS regions. A consistent reading is that a window both helps to infer the propagation state and, at a crossing, marks a place on the map where the signal strength steps; the models were not examined to confirm either mechanism. The gain is positive in every one of the $20$ splits for both ensembles, and with half of the devices single-antenna, which gives $14$ to $22$ test users per split, it is $1.20 \pm 0.15$ and $1.42 \pm 0.15\,\text{m}$.

\subsection{A Blocked Line of Sight}
\label{subsec:blocked}

% src: CONTINUE_HERE.md §4 (prediction: advantage grows with blockage duration until the pre-blockage heading stops being informative, then decays); followup_2026_10/blocked_los/history_mechanism_followup_summary.json (20 seeds, 360 users, h in {0,5}; multi-antenna XGBoost full: blocked +6.29+-0.18 m (36.1%), unblocked +6.66+-0.15 (38.7%); RF +6.13+-0.18 / +6.16+-0.12; by width XGB +5.24, +5.62, +5.66, +6.04, +5.95, +5.27, +6.11, +7.65 m = 30.2, 32.0, 32.9, 35.3, 35.1, 32.3, 35.1, 41.1%; angle_mean blocked +6.51+-0.18; single-antenna blocked +0.47+-0.54, unblocked +0.29+-0.36); superseded five-seed pooled reading in blocked_los_2026_09
The blocked line-of-sight scenario was designed to test whether history becomes more valuable as a blockage lengthens, up to the point where the heading observed before the blockage stops being informative, after which its value should decay. Over $20$ splits, with the two device groups analysed separately, history lowered the error of multi-antenna devices by $6.29 \pm 0.18\,\text{m}$ ($36\%$) inside the blocked stretch and by $6.66 \pm 0.15\,\text{m}$ ($39\%$) outside it for XGBoost, and by $6.13 \pm 0.18$ and $6.16 \pm 0.12\,\text{m}$ for Random Forest. The blocked stretch is therefore not special. The reduction does not rise and fall with the width of the blocked strip~(Figure~\ref{fig:blocked}): it lies between $5.2$ and $6.1\,\text{m}$ from $5$ to $120\,\text{m}$ and reaches $7.7\,\text{m}$ at the widest strip, $150\,\text{m}$, so the predicted dependence on blockage length does not appear. Single-antenna devices gain nothing detectable, $0.47 \pm 0.54\,\text{m}$ inside the stretch for XGBoost.

The size of the gain has the same source as in the macro cell. The serving BS stands $500\,\text{m}$ from the corridor, so an azimuth step of $5^\circ$ spans tens of meters across it, and the unordered mean of the angle window recovers all of the gain~($6.51 \pm 0.18\,\text{m}$ inside the stretch). The scenario shows that history keeps its value through a blockage of any length tested, but it does not isolate an effect of blockage, because the same gain appears outside the blocked stretch.

\subsection{Without the Interfering Base Stations}
\label{subsec:no-interference}

% src: followup_2026_10/no_interference/{uniform_nlos,mixed_propagation}/history_mechanism_followup_summary.json (20 seeds, SINR recomputed as rss - antenna_gain_db + 104 dB, i.e. p_int = 0 in the runners' sinr = p_sig/(p_int + p_noise), p_noise = -104 dBm); median SINR -3.0 dB (uniform) / -2.6 dB (mixed); median SNR - SINR 34.5 / 48.4 dB
Both macro-cell realizations contain two interfering BSs, and the cell is fully interference-limited: median SINR is $-3\,\text{dB}$, against a median interference-free signal-to-noise ratio of about $32$ to $46\,\text{dB}$. The interferers enter only through SINR, so switching them off is the same simulation with SINR replaced by that ratio, which follows exactly from the stored RSS; no new channel simulation is needed. Table~\ref{tab:no-interference} gives the result for twenty user splits. For multi-antenna devices the interferers are worth between $0.6$ and $1.0\,\text{m}$ without history, and the history gain is slightly larger without them, by $0.3$ to $0.4\,\text{m}$; angle history still carries all of it, $3.50 \pm 0.18\,\text{m}$ of the $3.44\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost on uniform NLOS. For single-antenna devices removing them changes the error by under $1\,\text{m}$ on uniform NLOS and costs $2.9\,\text{m}$ on mixed data, where the history gain of the previous subsection survives unchanged~($1.04 \pm 0.23$ against $1.14 \pm 0.29\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost). The conclusions of this report therefore do not depend on the interfering stations. Placing them elsewhere was not tried.
