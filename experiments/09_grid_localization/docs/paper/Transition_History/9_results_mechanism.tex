\section{Results: What History Reads}
\label{sec:results-mechanism}

% src: diagnostics_2026_10/{baseline_uniform_nlos,mixed_propagation}/history_mechanism_diagnostic_summary.json (5 seeds 42/1/7/13/99; models xgboost, random_forest, knn; conditions pure_snap, snap, rss_hist, angle_hist, ray_hist, full; gains seed-paired against snap); followup_2026_10/{uniform_nlos,mixed_propagation} (20 seeds, xgboost and random_forest)
In the macro-cell study, the gain from history is carried almost entirely by the angle channels, and what it mainly reduces is range error. The test fed $k$-NN, Random Forest and XGBoost history for one group of channels at a time while keeping every other channel at its current value: the RSS group~(RSS, SINR and the RSS difference), the angle group~(the sines and cosines of both angles and the azimuth difference), or the ray group~(the two ray-projection coordinates and their differences). All conditions use the same samples and the same user splits as the full $h=5$ input, so every comparison is paired, and none of these models selects anything on the test users. The test ran on both the uniform-NLOS and the mixed-propagation realizations, first over five splits for the three models and then over $20$ splits for XGBoost and Random Forest. Section~\ref{subsec:averaging} extends it to the four neural models and to what the angle history contains.

The first prediction holds without exception~(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism-conditions}). For multi-antenna devices on the uniform-NLOS data, angle history alone lowered error by $3.28 \pm 0.27\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost and $3.14 \pm 0.30\,\text{m}$ for Random Forest, as much as history on every channel~($3.24$ and $2.79\,\text{m}$). RSS history alone gave $0.25 \pm 0.14\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost and nothing detectable for Random Forest, and ray history behaved the same way. For $k$-NN, angle history alone outperformed the full window~($5.19$ against $3.79\,\text{m}$), and RSS history made it worse by $2.27\,\text{m}$, consistent with uninformative lags adding noise to its distance metric. The mixed-propagation data repeat the pattern, with angle-only gains of $2.4$ to $3.6\,\text{m}$ across the three models. Over $20$ splits the numbers hold: angle history alone gives $3.16$ and $3.06\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost and Random Forest, the full window $3.15$ and $2.75\,\text{m}$, and RSS history $0.23$ and $-0.22\,\text{m}$. The four neural networks, given history one channel group at a time by holding the other channels at their current value, show the same pattern over five splits: angle history alone lowers multi-antenna error by $3.0$ to $3.4\,\text{m}$, the full window by $2.9$ to $3.2\,\text{m}$, and RSS history by under $0.2\,\text{m}$. The angle-channel result therefore covers all seven model families.

% src: per_seed.xgboost.{snap,full}.groups.multi radial_mean / tangential_mean (5-seed means 17.31 -> 14.12, 5.02 -> 4.60); single 20.23 -> 20.13, 25.22 -> 25.29; followup_2026_10/uniform_nlos summary.xgboost.{snap,full}.multi.shape (20 seeds: radial 17.21 -> 14.06, tangential 4.96 -> 4.61)
What the angle history improves is mainly the estimate of distance~(Figure~\ref{fig:radial-tangential}). For multi-antenna devices, five steps of history lowered XGBoost's mean radial error from $17.3$ to $14.1\,\text{m}$ and its tangential error only from $5.0$ to $4.6\,\text{m}$; the same holds over $20$ splits and for Random Forest and the neural models. Bearing was already well determined by the current angle measurement; the window improves range. Single-antenna devices show no change in either component. Section~\ref{subsec:averaging} asks why a window of angles carries range.

The test also measures how much history the $h=0$ input already contains. Removing the first differences from the $h=0$ input raised multi-antenna error by $0.49 \pm 0.14\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost and $0.40 \pm 0.21\,\text{m}$ for Random Forest. The history gains reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-multi} are therefore measured against a baseline that already holds one step of change, and they understate the full effect of history by roughly $15\%$.

The second prediction holds on the uniform-NLOS data and fails on the mixed-propagation data. Under uniform NLOS, no channel group lowered single-antenna error for any model over five splits; every confidence interval includes zero. Over $20$ splits XGBoost's gains are $0.02 \pm 0.37\,\text{m}$ for RSS history and $-0.18 \pm 0.34\,\text{m}$ for the full window, while Random Forest's full-window gain, $0.42 \pm 0.39\,\text{m}$, only just clears zero. Raising the share of single-antenna devices to $50\%$, which gives $14$ to $22$ single-antenna test users per split, XGBoost gains $0.30 \pm 0.24\,\text{m}$ and Random Forest $0.18 \pm 0.24\,\text{m}$ with the full window. Any gain without an angle is therefore no larger than about $1\%$ of the error. With mixed propagation, RSS history lowered single-antenna error for both tree ensembles, by $1.36 \pm 0.26\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost and $1.99 \pm 0.66\,\text{m}$ for Random Forest over five splits and by $1.28 \pm 0.29$ and $1.41 \pm 0.36\,\text{m}$ over $20$, positive in every one of the $20$ splits. The separate five-seed comparison of all seven models on the mixed data confirms this for the two tree ensembles. The neural models share it only weakly: RSS history alone lowers single-antenna error by $0.6 \pm 0.4\,\text{m}$ for the GRU and $0.9 \pm 0.8\,\text{m}$ for the attention network, and the full window gives none of the four a gain beyond its interval. Section~\ref{sec:results-propagation} examines where this gain arises.

The third prediction is not supported, and for XGBoost it is better described as inconclusive~(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism-by-range}). If RSS history were limited by the shrinking gradient of Equation~(\ref{eq:rss-gradient}), its gain should be largest near the BS and fall with range. For multi-antenna devices on the uniform-NLOS data, XGBoost's RSS-only gain over $20$ splits does fall from $0.9\,\text{m}$ within $40\,\text{m}$ to slightly below zero at $80$ to $120\,\text{m}$, but it rises again to $0.7\,\text{m}$ beyond $120\,\text{m}$, and Random Forest's gain has no trend. For single-antenna devices on the mixed data the RSS-only gain is flat across the four range bands at $0.9$ to $1.8\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost over $20$ splits; the rise from $0.4$ to $2.4\,\text{m}$ seen over five splits did not replicate. The range-dependent account of Section~\ref{subsec:hypothesis} therefore does not explain when RSS history helps. Its other half, that the macro-cell gain depends on the angle channels, is the part the test confirms.

The angle-only gain is itself strongly range-dependent. For multi-antenna devices over $20$ splits it falls from about $11\,\text{m}$ within $40\,\text{m}$ of the BS to under $2\,\text{m}$ at $80$ to $120\,\text{m}$ and rises again to $5.4\,\text{m}$ beyond $120\,\text{m}$, following the error that is left to remove ($22.5$, $21.3$, $14.4$ and $28.2\,\text{m}$ without history). In the nearest band it is entirely elevation: $11.0\,\text{m}$ of the $11.0\,\text{m}$. This is what the next subsection explains.

\subsection{What the Angle History Contains}
\label{subsec:averaging}

% src: followup_2026_10/uniform_nlos (20 seeds, XGBoost / RF, multi-antenna, Student-t): full 3.15+-0.12 / 2.75+-0.08; angle_hist 3.16+-0.14 / 3.06+-0.13; angle_mean 3.38+-0.19 / 3.61+-0.19; angle_shuffled 2.93+-0.12 / 2.66+-0.13; el_hist 2.72+-0.13 / 2.24+-0.12; az_hist 0.63+-0.08 / 0.77+-0.05; rss_hist 0.23+-0.09 / -0.22+-0.11; paired MAE(angle_hist)-MAE(angle_mean) +0.22+-0.11 / +0.55+-0.08, MAE(angle_hist)-MAE(angle_shuffled) -0.23+-0.05 / -0.40+-0.02
Section~\ref{subsec:history-dof} gives a geometric reason why a window of angles should carry range: for a given sideways movement the bearing turns at the rate $v_\perp/r$. A simpler, statistical reason is also available. Every angle report is the true angle plus an independent $4^\circ$ error, quantized to $5^\circ$ steps, so a window offers several noisy views of an angle that has barely changed, and averaging them gives a better estimate. Four tests separate the two, on $20$ splits for XGBoost and Random Forest~(Figure~\ref{fig:angle-decomposition}). Replacing the window by the unordered mean of each angle channel gives $3.38 \pm 0.19\,\text{m}$ and $3.61 \pm 0.19\,\text{m}$, as much as the ordered history~($3.16$ and $3.06\,\text{m}$), so the order of the reports is not needed. Shuffling the order of the lagged reports keeps $2.93$ and $2.66\,\text{m}$, a loss of only $0.23 \pm 0.05$ and $0.40 \pm 0.02\,\text{m}$. Elevation history alone gives $2.72$ and $2.24\,\text{m}$ and azimuth history alone $0.63$ and $0.77\,\text{m}$. The base station stands roughly $8.5\,\text{m}$ above a device, so elevation encodes distance, and averaging it is a way of improving the range estimate; this is why radial error is what falls.

The decisive test removes the error. With exact arrival angles the multi-antenna error without history falls to $2.3\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost and $1.6\,\text{m}$ for Random Forest, and history gives $0.00 \pm 0.11$ and $-1.02 \pm 0.03\,\text{m}$: there is nothing left to average. The four neural models behave alike, with errors of $1.8$ to $3.2\,\text{m}$ and a change of $-0.5$ to $-0.8\,\text{m}$ when history is added. Quantization alone, with no noise, leaves the error at $14.2\,\text{m}$ and still gives a gain of $1.9$ and $2.3\,\text{m}$; noise alone gives $3.1\,\text{m}$~(Figure~\ref{fig:aoa-error-model}a). The history gain of Section~\ref{sec:results-multi} is thus not a property of bearing geometry. It is a property of averaging imperfect angle reports.

% src: followup_2026_10/aoa_impairment/corr_rho{0,0.5,0.9,0.99} (10 seeds; XGB full +3.10+-0.23, +1.79+-0.13, +0.98+-0.14, +1.16+-0.16; elevation +2.69, +1.27, +0.25, +0.03; azimuth +0.63, +0.49, +0.69, +0.92; RF full +2.75, +1.87, +1.15, +1.26); neural_corr_rho0.9 (+0.34 to +0.61)
How much of the gain remains depends on how the error behaves from one report to the next. If the angle error persists across the window, averaging cannot remove it. Making the $4^\circ$ error an autoregressive sequence along each walk, XGBoost's gain falls from $3.10 \pm 0.23\,\text{m}$ with independent errors to $1.79 \pm 0.13$, $0.98 \pm 0.14$ and $1.16 \pm 0.16\,\text{m}$ at lag-1 correlations of $0.5$, $0.9$ and $0.99$, and Random Forest's from $2.75$ to $1.26\,\text{m}$~(Figure~\ref{fig:aoa-error-model}b). The elevation part disappears first~($2.69$ to $0.03\,\text{m}$ for XGBoost); what remains, about $1\,\text{m}$ or $5$ to $7\%$ of the error, is in azimuth. It is consistent with the device's motion carrying the true angle across quantization steps, which a window resolves more finely than a single report, but that account was not tested separately. The neural models keep only $0.3$ to $0.6\,\text{m}$ at a correlation of $0.9$, about $2$ to $3\%$. The headline gain of $15$ to $21\%$ is therefore specific to angle errors that are independent between reports, which is how the macro-cell simulation draws them; how independent real angle estimates are was not measured here.

% src: followup_2026_10/history_depth/h{1,3,10,20} (10 seeds) and uniform_nlos (h=5, 20 seeds), XGBoost multi-antenna full history +1.21, +2.53, +3.15, +3.51, +3.68; angle_mean +1.26, +2.72, +3.38, +3.16, +1.39; angle_hist at h=20 +3.59
The same account explains how the gain depends on window length. Over $10$ to $20$ splits, XGBoost's multi-antenna gain from the full window is $1.2$, $2.5$, $3.2$, $3.5$ and $3.7\,\text{m}$ at $1$, $3$, $5$, $10$ and $20$ steps ($4$ to $80\,\text{m}$ of travel), so one extra report is already worth $1.2\,\text{m}$ and most of the gain is in place by $10$ steps. The unordered mean of the angle window is the better input up to $5$ steps~($3.38\,\text{m}$) and then fails~($1.39\,\text{m}$ at $20$ steps) because over $80\,\text{m}$ the true angle has changed and an unweighted mean is biased; the ordered window still gives $3.6\,\text{m}$ there, since the model can weight recent reports. At long windows the order of the reports does matter, as a way of discounting stale ones.
